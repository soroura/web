Builds

Different tools. One connected programme of work.

These projects help health teams learn, prepare, exercise, assess, and improve. Some are active platforms; others are research or programme infrastructure. Each begins with a problem observed in practice.

SimReady

In active development

Simulation exercises are among the best ways to test preparedness, yet much of the work is still coordinated through documents, spreadsheets, messaging groups, and memory. SimReady explores how one platform can support the exercise lifecycle from design and conduct to evaluation and learning.

The aim is not to automate the judgement of exercise teams. It is to make scenarios, injects, coordination, observations, and lessons easier to manage and carry forward.

Collaboration sought: exercise practitioners, pilot teams, evaluators, and specialists in offline-first and multilingual systems.

AI-Augmented Offline-First WHO Methodology Arabic RTL Human-Accountable

Assessment and Improvement Tools

Prototyping and programme development

Assessments often produce a score and a report, then lose momentum. This work explores how health-facility readiness, safety, emergency-care, and resilience instruments can move from data collection into prioritised actions and visible follow-up.

The focus is the full improvement loop: understand the gap, agree what matters, assign action, follow progress, and retain evidence for the next review.

Collaboration sought: hospital teams, quality and preparedness leads, assessment specialists, and implementation researchers.

Health Facility Assessment WHO-Aligned Quality Improvement National Scale

Emergency-Care Learning Infrastructure

Programme work and product exploration

Training completion is not the same as capability. This work examines how emergency-care programmes can better support trainers, local delivery, practice, evidence, and continued improvement after a course ends.

Collaboration sought: trainers, programme leads, learners, curriculum specialists, and teams evaluating changes in practice.

Emergency Care Learning Systems Trainer Support Evaluation

MSc Dissertation: TraumaApp ED Data Analysis

In Progress · University of Edinburgh

A work-based dissertation analysing real-life data from the TraumaApp pilot at a Scottish hospital. The TraumaApp captures timed steps of the emergency department journey with a granularity that traditional registries cannot provide — making it possible to examine the factors that affect ED length of stay and time to definitive care.

Collaboration sought: emergency-care researchers and practitioners interested in operational data, time-to-care measures, and service improvement.

Data Science Emergency Department Time-to-Care Analysis

Supporting work: Related work includes mass-casualty and EMT exercise tools, hospital resilience methods, workforce planning, curriculum design, horizon scanning, and reusable knowledge and design systems.
