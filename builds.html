Builds

I build tools to learn. These are the first three.

Each one started with a piece of work that was slower or harder than it needed to be. They are pilots I designed and built myself, with AI-assisted development. They are not WHO products, and nothing on this page shows real patient, participant or facility data. More will follow, one at a time, as each is ready to show.

PSFH: Patient Safety Friendly Hospital assessment

Working platform, piloted in two hospitals
Health systems, hospital quality and patient safety

The problem

The WHO Patient Safety Friendly Hospital Initiative asks assessors to rate 134 criteria across 21 standards. In practice this runs on spreadsheets and paper, so consolidating scores, linking evidence and following up on gaps is manual work.

What I built

An offline-first platform that scores as the team works, applies the critical-criteria gate, produces exit reports, and turns each gap into an action with a priority and a due date. A portfolio view shows where facilities stand over time.

Where it stands

Piloted in two hospitals. Organisational rollout is not confirmed yet, and results are still to be written up.

The initiative and its framework are WHO's, and the programme belongs to the people who run it. This platform is my independent build on top of them, not a WHO product and not WHO-endorsed. Demonstrations use fictitious facilities and illustrative scores.

PSFPC: Patient Safety Friendly Primary Care assessment

Working platform, piloted in one facility
Health systems, primary health care

The problem

The same spreadsheet problem, in primary care, where a peer team of two or three visits a facility and connectivity cannot be relied on.

What I built

A phone-first assessment for the WHO Eastern Mediterranean primary care framework: 125 criteria across 19 standards. Work continues offline, and no patient data is kept on the device. A critical criterion is either met or it is not; the tool never gives partial credit.

Where it stands

Piloted in one primary care facility; results to add. One scoring threshold is an interim ruling by the programme, not a WHO rule, and is labelled that way.

BEC Hub Egypt: Basic Emergency Care course operations

Developed
Emergency care, training and credentialing

The problem

Running a national Basic Emergency Care programme meant course requests, trainer and provider lists, and certificates spread across spreadsheets and shared files.

What I built

One place for course requests and accreditation, provider and trainer registers, credentialing, and certificates that anyone can verify, with dashboards at country level.

Where it stands

Developed for Egypt's Basic Emergency Care programme, which I coordinate with the African Federation for Emergency Medicine. The programme and its curriculum belong to WHO and AFEM.
