Better preparedness needs better connections between learning, practice, evidence, and improvement.

Health teams are asked to prepare for extraordinary situations with fragmented tools: courses that end at certification, exercises coordinated on paper, assessments trapped in spreadsheets, and lessons that are difficult to carry into the next response. My work starts inside that gap — working with practitioners to turn recurring field problems into useful tools, methods, and shared infrastructure.
