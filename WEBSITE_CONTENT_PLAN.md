# soroura.org Content Update Plan

**Status:** Draft for review  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 11 July 2026  
**Planning basis:** `background-1.md` and the current website

## 1. Objective

Update soroura.org so it does three things clearly:

1. explains the larger idea connecting Ahmed Sorour's work;
2. demonstrates that idea through a focused, credible portfolio;
3. invites specific people to contribute, test, research, or collaborate.

The website should remain a small, fast, static personal site. This is a content and information-architecture update, not a platform rebuild.

## 2. Proposed outcome

A visitor should understand, within one short visit:

- the problem Ahmed works on;
- the system of work connecting his projects;
- the experience that gives him a credible perspective;
- what is already being built or tested;
- what kind of collaboration is useful now;
- how to begin that collaboration.

## 3. Proposed positioning

### Primary statement

> Better preparedness needs better connections between learning, practice, evidence, and improvement.

### Supporting explanation

The work connects emergency-care learning, simulation, readiness assessment, evidence, and improvement. Each project starts with a recurring field problem: training that is difficult to scale, exercises managed through paper and messaging apps, assessments trapped in spreadsheets, or evidence that cannot be turned into timely action.

### Organising model

**Learn → Prepare → Exercise → Assess → Improve**

This is proposed as the main conceptual structure for the site. It describes a connected programme of work without implying that every project is part of one product.

### Collaboration promise

> Bring a real problem, a setting where it matters, or expertise that can make the work more rigorous.

## 4. Audiences

### Primary

- emergency-care and disaster-medicine practitioners;
- health ministries, public-health agencies, and hospital leaders;
- preparedness, simulation, EMT, training, and quality-improvement teams;
- potential field and institutional collaborators.

### Secondary

- health-service and implementation researchers;
- product designers, software engineers, data scientists, and responsible-AI practitioners;
- organisations able to pilot, evaluate, fund, or scale useful work;
- conference organisers and professional networks.

### Audience needs

| Audience | What they need to see | Desired action |
|---|---|---|
| Practitioner | Recognition of a real operational problem | Share a use case or review a tool |
| Programme lead | Relevance, maturity, and implementation fit | Discuss a pilot or programme integration |
| Researcher | Clear questions, methods, and evidence gaps | Propose evaluation or joint research |
| Technical collaborator | Defined constraints and bounded problems | Contribute specialised technical work |
| Institution or funder | Credible portfolio, governance, and pathway to impact | Explore support or partnership |
| Conference organiser | Distinct perspective and public evidence | Invite a talk, panel, or workshop |

## 5. Scope

### In scope

- Verify and organise the public project portfolio.
- Rewrite the homepage around the larger programme of work.
- Reorganise the Builds page around problem areas and project maturity.
- Tighten the About page and add a forward-looking synthesis.
- Reorganise Field Notes so it contains thinking, not only activity listings.
- Turn the contact experience into a specific collaboration invitation.
- Update metadata to reflect approved page messages.
- Make the minimum HTML and CSS changes required by the new content.
- Review all pages locally before deployment.

### Out of scope for this iteration

- A framework, CMS, database, or build process.
- User accounts or an online collaboration portal.
- A complete visual redesign.
- Publishing confidential or institutionally owned materials.
- Claiming deployments, impact, affiliations, ownership, or product maturity without verification.
- Adding every workspace project to the public portfolio.

## 6. Content principles

All new copy will follow the existing brand voice:

- Lead with a concrete field problem.
- Show the work instead of advertising it.
- Use specific evidence only when verified.
- Write in the first person.
- Keep paragraphs and pages concise.
- Avoid startup language, superlatives, and vague claims.
- Explain specialist terms on first use.
- Treat AI as an assistant; people remain responsible for decisions.
- Distinguish personal, academic, WHO, and collaborative work.
- End sections with a meaningful next step.

## 7. Portfolio structure

2. **Exercises and readiness**
3. **Assessment and improvement**
4. **Evidence and decisions**
5. **Shared knowledge and design infrastructure**

### Project publication levels

| Research | Academic or evidence-generating work | Builds and/or Field Notes |

## 8. Page plans

## 8.1 Home

### Job of the page

Explain the idea, prove that it is real, and lead the right visitor toward a relevant project, note, or collaboration route.

### Proposed content order

1. **Hero** — field problem, positioning, and primary action.
2. **The connected cycle** — Learn, Prepare, Exercise, Assess, Improve.
3. **Flagship work** — three to five approved projects.
4. **How the work is approached** — field-led, context-aware, human-accountable, measurable.
5. **Current field notes** — substantive writing rather than event listings.
6. **Why Ahmed** — concise frontline, systems, research, and building synthesis.
7. **Collaboration invitation** — who, what, and how.

### Proposed calls to action

- Primary: **Explore the work**
- Secondary: **Collaborate on a real problem**
- Supporting: **Read the field notes**

### Remove or reduce

- Generic repetition of “building tools for the field.”
- Three old activity cards presented as recent content.
- Homepage claims that cannot be verified.
- Repeated About-page copy.

### Acceptance criteria

- The main idea is understandable without visiting About.
- The portfolio is visible without becoming a product catalogue.
- At least one clear collaboration route appears before the footer.
- All featured claims have an approved evidence source.

## 8.2 Builds

### Job of the page

Show the portfolio as connected work, while being honest about maturity and what each project needs next.

### Proposed project-card structure

- Project name and one-line purpose
- The field problem
- Intended users
- What exists now
- Current stage
- Evidence or learning
- What is next
- Collaboration sought
- Public link or demonstration, if available

### Proposed status vocabulary

- Exploring
- Prototyping
- In active development
- Piloting
- In use
- Research in progress
- Archived

Avoid “shipped” unless the release and user context makes the term meaningful.

### Acceptance criteria

- Every public project has a verified owner, status, and role statement.
- Projects are grouped by problem area.
- Visitors can distinguish a concept, prototype, pilot, and operational tool.
- Every flagship project has a specific next step or collaboration request.

## 8.3 About

### Job of the page

Explain how Ahmed developed this perspective and why his experience spans field practice, system implementation, research, and building.

### Proposed changes

- Retain the three arcs: frontline, systems, building.
- Shorten the detailed chronology in the main narrative.
- Keep a compact timeline for evidence.
- Add **What I am trying to build now**.
- Clarify the boundary between personal work and institutional responsibilities.
- Review all dates, titles, qualifications, and role descriptions.

### Acceptance criteria

- The story reads as one trajectory rather than a CV.
- Institutional affiliations are accurate and appropriately framed.
- The page ends with present direction and collaboration, not credentials.

## 8.4 Field Notes

### Job of the page

Show the reasoning, methods, observations, and learning behind the work.

### Proposed categories

- Field observations
- Build notes
- Methods and tools
- Research notes
- Talks, training, and publications

### Proposed first-note backlog

| Priority | Working title | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | From fragmented tools to a preparedness cycle | Introduce the larger idea behind the portfolio |
| 2 | Why simulation exercises still run on paper | Establish a flagship problem through field experience |
| 3 | What an assessment must do after the score | Connect assessment to improvement |
| 4 | AI should suggest; humans should decide | State the responsible-AI position |
| 5 | Offline-first is an operational requirement | Explain a recurring design constraint |
| 6 | What training completion fails to tell us | Connect learning to capability and performance |

### Acceptance criteria

- Engagements are no longer presented as if they are long-form notes.
- At least two substantive notes are ready when the new structure goes live.
- Each note connects a field observation to a useful implication.

## 8.5 Collaborate

### Job of the page

Turn general interest into useful, bounded conversations.

### Proposed navigation label

**Collaborate**

This would replace **Say Hello**, subject to review.

### Proposed sections

- Problems worth discussing
- Who Ahmed wants to hear from
- Ways to collaborate
- What to include in a first message
- LinkedIn contact route
- Institutional boundary note

### Suggested first-message prompts

- “We run this workflow and this is where it breaks.”
- “We could test this with this team or setting.”
- “I can contribute expertise in this specific area.”
- “I am studying this question and see a possible research collaboration.”

### Acceptance criteria

- The page does more than display a social profile.
- LinkedIn is the only published direct-contact route.
- Visitors understand that collaboration begins with a concrete problem or contribution.

## 9. Cross-page requirements

- Navigation must remain identical across all HTML pages.
- Active navigation state must remain correct.
- Footer and copyright must remain consistent.
- Page titles, descriptions, Open Graph text, canonical links, and structured data must match approved content.
- Public claims must be sourced in the planning inventory, even if sources are not displayed on the page.
- Pages must work in light and dark mode.
- Mobile navigation and responsive layouts must be checked.
- No new build step, framework, or bundler.
- If `style.css` changes, increment its version query consistently in every HTML file.

## 12. Review sequence

We should review the plan in this order:

1. **Positioning:** Is the proposed central idea accurate?
2. **Portfolio boundary:** What belongs publicly on this personal site?
3. **Flagships:** Which three to five projects best prove the idea?
4. **Audience priority:** Whose collaboration matters most now?
5. **Invitation:** What exact contribution or partnership is being requested?
6. **Page scope:** Are the proposed roles and sections right?
7. **Delivery:** Is the release sequence proportionate?

## 13. Decisions for the first review

### D1 — Central idea

**Proposal:** Use **Learn → Prepare → Exercise → Assess → Improve** to organise the work.

**Review:** Approve, revise, or replace.

### D2 — Primary identity

**Proposal:** Lead with the shared field problem — “Better preparedness needs better connections between learning, practice, evidence, and improvement” — then introduce Ahmed's public-health, disaster-medicine, and builder perspective as part of the response.

**Review:** Decide whether “emergencies” is the right boundary or whether the scope should explicitly include broader health-system improvement.

### D3 — Public portfolio size

**Proposal:** Feature three to five flagship projects and place a limited number of supporting projects on Builds.

**Review:** Approve the principle before selecting projects.

### D4 — Collaboration priority

**Proposal:** Prioritise field partners, pilot settings, subject-matter review, and research evaluation before broad funding or general networking requests.

**Review:** Rank the collaboration types needed in the next six months.

### D5 — Contact page

**Proposal:** Rename **Say Hello** to **Collaborate** and qualify the invitation.

**Review:** Approve or retain the current label.

### D6 — Field Notes launch threshold

**Proposal:** Do not launch the reorganised Field Notes page until at least two substantive notes are ready.

**Review:** Approve or choose a different threshold.
